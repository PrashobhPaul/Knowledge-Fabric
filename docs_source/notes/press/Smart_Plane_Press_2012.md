# The smart plane in the Telugu press, 2012

Two Telugu newspapers wrote about Prashobh Paul's B.Tech project at Christu Jyothi Institute of
Technology and Science, the smart plane, in 2012: Eenadu, in its Jangaon edition, under the
headline "The little plane flew", and Namasthe Telangana, on Monday 24 September 2012, page 8,
under "With the sky as the limit". Eenadu calls it the Smart Plane Climate Analyser Robot and
prints its specifications: a 4-stroke engine of 0.6 cc, 20 percent nitro fuel, 8 kg, a body of
balsa and plywood.

How this note was made: Prashobh Paul uploaded photographs of the two clippings in November
2026, and they were transcribed from the Telugu. The Telugu headlines are copied as printed;
everything else is translated. The two sections below are what the papers reported, not his
account; his own account, in his words, is in the last section.

## Eenadu, Jangaon edition: "The little plane flew"

- Newspaper: Eenadu, Jangaon edition, filed by its "News Today" desk.
- Date and page of the issue: not in the transcription.
- Headline: ఎగిరిన బుల్లి విమానం ("The little plane flew").
- Photo caption: కళాశాల పరిసరాల్లో ఎగురుతున్న వాతావరణ విశ్లేషక.. రోబో విమానం ("The weather-analyser robot plane flying over the college premises").

### What Eenadu reported

As reported by Eenadu: students of Christu Jyothi Institute of Technology and Science, in
Yeshwanthapur, Jangaon mandal, developed the "Smart Plane Climate Analyser Robot" under the
college's research and development (R&D) department, and it was unveiled on Saturday,
Engineers' Day.

As reported by Eenadu, in a box headed స్మార్ట్ ప్లేన్ ప్రత్యేకతలివి ("These are the smart
plane's special features"), the specifications of the smart plane are:

- Engine: 4-stroke, with a capacity of 0.6 cc, as printed.
- Fuel: 20 percent nitro.
- Sensors: the box lists the sensors the plane carries; the list is not transcribed here.
- Dimensions: 6.2 ft and 6.5 ft, as printed; this transcription does not say which figure is which.
- Weight: 8 kg.
- Body material: balsa and plywood.

As reported by Eenadu: the smart plane was unveiled by Acharya (Professor) Mallareddy of
Kakatiya University, with Dr. Narayana Reddy, founder of the St. Peter's institutions, and
Raja, deputy manager of the Warangal Diocese.

As reported by Eenadu, under the heading మంత్రి పొన్నాల ప్రశంసలు ("Minister Ponnala's
praise"): Minister Ponnala praised the students and congratulated them by phone.

As reported by Eenadu: the report also names the college director, Papireddy, and the
principal, Sunil Pratap Reddy. Both names are transcribed from the Telugu, and their English
spelling is uncertain.

## Namasthe Telangana, Monday 24 September 2012, page 8: "With the sky as the limit"

- Newspaper: Namasthe Telangana.
- Date: Monday, 24 September 2012.
- Page: 8.
- Headline: ఆకాశమే హద్దుగా.. ("With the sky as the limit").

### What Namasthe Telangana reported

As reported by Namasthe Telangana: a feature on the remarkable innovations of the students of
Christu Jyothi Institute of Technology and Science, with profiles of the students and photos of
their projects, the smart plane among them, along with other student projects such as a
solar-powered bicycle.

The copy on record is not sharp enough to read which of the student profiles name Prashobh
Paul, so this note does not say.

## What is certain and what is not

- The Eenadu transcription carries no issue date, and the report says only that the plane was
  unveiled on "Saturday, Engineers' Day". Engineers' Day in India is 15 September, and
  15 September 2012 was a Saturday, so the unveiling was most likely on 15 September 2012. That
  date is inferred, not printed.
- The names are transliterated from the Telugu. The spelling of the director (Papireddy) and
  the principal (Sunil Pratap Reddy) is uncertain.
- The sensor list, and which of the two dimensions is the length and which the wingspan, are not
  transcribed.
- Which Namasthe Telangana student profiles name Prashobh Paul cannot be read.

## How the papers fit my own account

The papers describe what the college and its guests saw that day: a climate-analyser robot
plane, the Smart Plane Climate Analyser Robot, built by the students of Christu Jyothi under the
college's R&D department and flown over the campus on Engineers' Day. They credit the students
as a group.

What the papers do not describe is the part that carried me into AI: flying the plane by
voice, with speech recognition I built on Hidden Markov Models. That is my own account, not the
papers'. I had been working on voice-controlled systems in 2011 and 2012, because I wanted a
machine that would read my textbooks aloud, and Dr. Uma N. Dulhera backed the ambitious idea of
controlling a UAV by voice.

These cuttings are more valuable to me than any other record: they were my launchpad. The
recognition carried me into my M.Tech in Knowledge Engineering, and my job offer came partly
because of this project.
