# Prashobh Paul — in his own words

Answers written by Prashobh Paul for the Knowledge Fabric, November 2026. These are the
source of record for the questions below; the canon quotes them.

## Origins and schooling
I am from Thrissur, Kerala. My father taught in Kendriya Vidyalaya, so I studied in KV
schools too: mostly KV Thrissur, and from the 7th to the 9th standard in KV Ottapalam
because of my father's transfer.

## Why computer science, and then AI
In school I chose computer science over biology because I was interested in computer
games and thought that studying computer science would let me make games. In B.Tech I
chose it for my interest in building cool software and games. I was also drawn to
architecture and civil engineering, and I liked maths and physics, but I hated
chemistry, which was mandatory for B.Arch at the time.

My first paper on AI came in the second year of B.Tech, in 2010. Then came my interest
in voice-controlled systems based on Hidden Markov Models in 2011 and 2012. I built a
voice-controlled UAV, a climate-analyser smart plane that I flew by voice on Engineer's
Day in 2012. It got recognition and coverage in the regional Telugu papers, and I carried
that research into my M.Tech in Knowledge Engineering with a specialisation in AI. My job
offer landed during my M.Tech partly because of that project. Those paper cuttings are
more valuable to me than any other record: they were my launchpad.

## TCS: the first real taste of AI
TCS gave me the real-world taste of AI; until then I was on expert systems and
probabilistic predictions. After campus placement I had three months of training as a
regular IT engineer and was put into a client project. I started in Kochi, which was the
main office of the Digital Enterprise Systems and Solutions wing of TCS, with separate
labs for AI, Robotics, IoT and more. I formally emailed the zonal head and the digital
team head, copying RMG and HR, asking for a chance in the AI Labs. The lab had been formed
with senior leads and architects and an elite group of freshers from institutions like
the IITs and IIITs, and they rejected my request. After a month and a half of emails they
said I could join if I passed their interviews. Many people interviewed me for smaller
teams, and I kept knocking for another month until my sixteenth interview, with the
architect Manoj Madhavan. He gave me an NLP problem, what we would now call NLG, on
understanding W-H questions, and asked for my approach. He told me he was taking me into
his team not just for what I answered but for my relentless effort across those sixteen
interviews. RMG moved me officially into the team, on the condition that I would not get
the differential package the IIT freshers had.

From there I worked on NLP problems: custom OpenNLP models, an intelligent date tagger
that could infer indirect references to a day or date and was reused across many TCS
projects, a multi-class classification problem for Ultimatix, the in-house ticketing tool,
with the CTO team, and then type-ahead and intelligent prompting for advanced in-house
search tools with Manoj. I also spent a brief period in the Robotics lab under Dr. Roshy
and Rajaram, then Global Head for Robotics and Delivery Head of the Robotics Labs. TCS
taught me resilience and both the industrial and the research flavour of AI, and it gave me
the foundation of who I am.

After two years at TCS I moved to Primera Medical Technologies in Hyderabad, a sister
concern of Prime Healthcare in the US, where I worked for four years as NLP Engineer and
Senior NLP Engineer. One day I got a call from the same Dr. Roshy and Rajaram, who had moved
to a Hyderabad startup named Brane Enterprises as Director and Head of AI and Robotics,
asking if I would work with them again after a four-year gap. I joined as Associate
Solutions Leader in AI and Robotics, where I led projects like object detection, gait and
path planning and the physics engine for quadrupedal robots. That took my AI into robotics
and edge devices with ONNX, and in 2022 I also worked on vision glasses for the visually
impaired, a smaller cousin of the Meta Ray-Ban style glasses, as part of a Telangana State
Government initiative.

## What I am proud of
Having evolved along with AI itself: from basic NLP to transformers, LSTMs, LLMs and SLMs,
and now the agentic world. From a solutioning standpoint I am prouder of my approach than
of awards. When Rajaram assigned me an object detection problem whose accuracy had dropped
from 86 to 76 percent after the system went from 80 to 120 object classes, I had no prior
experience with RNNs or custom weight training, and YOLO was not helping on Indian
datasets. I took a hybrid approach from my NLP side: not just bounding boxes, but describing
the images and scenes, adding text-to-image tagging and a custom word2vec on top. That took
accuracy not just back to 86 but to 89.5. That out-of-the-box call, appreciated by Dr.
Roshy, who is known as the man who kick-started the driverless car in India, is worth more
to me than any award.

## The hardest problem
In healthcare it was solving the smaller problems better, making NLU and NLG better. I
worked a lot on the physician's note reader, which had to read notes for all inpatients
across more than sixty hospitals, each with at least a hundred beds, coming from Epic and
Meditech, some in HL7, some in MAR, collate them per patient across physician notes,
nursing notes and medication records, map them through ontologies like SNOMED, LOINC and
ICD-10, build custom relationship graphs and use them for real-time analytics. Lots of
small yet powerful NLP and ML on fairly huge data, for the first time, while acting as the
pillar of the NLP team for AI and Advanced Analytics at Primera. I am prouder of that than
of the sophisticated agentic work of today.

## Mentors
In B.Tech, Dr. Uma N. Dulhera, who supported my ambitious project of controlling a UAV by
voice. Then Manoj Madhavan, who saw my passion, made me strong in Python and helped with
fuzzy logic implementations. Then my role models, Roshy sir and Rajaram sir. Today I mentor
a team of young engineering graduates who joined Qualizeal as interns and freshers in the
AI Center of Excellence.

## How I choose frameworks, and what governance means
Choosing between LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen or custom orchestration depends on the
client's need and the situation. AI governance means a lot to me because AI adoption is
now in everything. On evaluating AI systems I have my own approaches and thoughts, not
only the products Qualizeal offers.

## What drives my decisions
Quality of work and challenges drive me, whether it is a job change or freelance work.

## Projects I am attached to
Now it is Knowledge Fabric and Knowledge Factory, which are not just agentic but a
knowledge layer for the AI-driven development life cycle of my company. Before that it was
the object detection problem, because of my NLP-based way of solving a vision problem, and
before that the college project that was my launchpad.

Invest.IQ is my work on using agentic AI in the Indian stock market. Koode exists because
my parents still worry when I travel; they do not like me doing the long drive from
Hyderabad to Thrissur and back. Pachaka Lokam came from wanting to help my wife and make my
own life easier by making sure I buy everything the kitchen needs during a busy day.

CogniFlow I made to produce architecture diagrams with motion and animation, which my
clients prefer over the traditional ones I used to draw in draw.io; even with Claude it
took a lot of prompting, so I built it and attached it to my Claude to make architecture
design work easier. I always build projects to make my life easier. Even the
voice-controlled systems in B.Tech began because I wanted something that would read PDF
textbooks aloud to me, since reading was boring for me and there were no audiobooks for
everything then. My work comes from working hard to avoid the things I do not like, in that
case reading.

## YouTube and travel
I started The Canvas of Earth to add my travel videos, because the artist in you can only
grow with a vision of both nature and culture. The trip that changed me most was Spiti:
the monasteries, understanding Om Mani Padme Hum, and the story behind the Tibetan prayer
flags on that very trip. My bucket list is to cover the various parts of India and study
our own culture better before travelling to see the world and cities like London or New
York. I shoot on a mobile and a DJI, and I edit mostly in Filmora.

## A typical day
AI Architect by day, a father by evening, and an artist at times on weekends.

## Teams I follow
Cricket is my favourite, since the Indian national team is better known for cricket than
football. Apart from the national cricket and football teams I love CSK in the IPL, Kerala
Blasters in the ISL, Manchester United in the Premier League, Brazil in the Copa and the
World Cup, and Spain in the Euros. In hockey it is the Indian national team, then the
Netherlands.

## What shapes my thinking
Conceptual art by many artists inspires me, and the poems of Wordsworth. I love art and
poems more than novels and stories.

## My art
Conceptual art is the category, and it is not modern art, so do not misunderstand it for
that. I love the name Phoenix because I draw and paint my feelings and like to make them a
bit metaphorical, as in poems.

## What I want my children to know about my work
Strive hard, and always follow the passion.
